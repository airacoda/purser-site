Two people, one household, one honest picture of the money.

Purser gathers every account a household holds, sorts what happened, and tells you plainly what is due before payday and what is safe to spend. An advisor named Penny watches the patterns so you do not have to.

What it does

The whole household on one screen

Today

Spending pace for the month, what is due before the next payday, and where the money went, in three glances.

Recurring

Every bill, subscription, card payment and paycheck the bank feed can see, with the ones that quietly changed price called out.

Upcoming

A payday view: income expected, bills due before it, and the number that matters, safe to spend.

Penny

Plain, short and warm. Penny notices a late fee, a duplicate charge or a card creeping toward its limit, and says so once.

Credit

Each person's credit reports, filed and read for the factors that move the score, kept separate from the shared money picture.

Apple Card

Each person's Apple Card joins the household picture from their own iPhone, with their own permission, through Apple's FinanceKit.

How it treats your data

Owned by the household, not by a company

Purser runs on a private cloud account that the household owns. Bank data arrives through connections each person authorises, stays encrypted, and is shown only to the members of that household. There are no advertisements, no analytics libraries and no data brokers, because there is no business model that needs them.

Money never moves through Purser. It reads, sorts and advises; every payment and transfer stays in your bank's own app, made by your own hand.

Purser is distributed privately, by invitation, to the members of one household. It is not offered to the public.
